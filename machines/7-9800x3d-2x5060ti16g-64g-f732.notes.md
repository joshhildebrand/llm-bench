This box is **VRAM-rich and GPU-bound**: 32 GB total across two RTX 5060 Ti (one on a PCIe **x1**
slot). Unlike the 8 GB box, a Q4-class quant + 128k KV **fits entirely in VRAM**, so decode is bound
by GPU memory bandwidth, not RAM — hence ~100 tok/s vs ~20.

**Recommended (sweet spot): `IQ4_XS` or `Q4_K_S`, 128k, fully GPU-resident** — flash on, KV q8_0,
MTP draft 4, threads 8, all experts on GPU (`cpu_experts=0`, `kv_to_gpu=true`). Ready-made preset:
[`presets/qwen3.6-iq4xs-dualgpu.json`](../presets/qwen3.6-iq4xs-dualgpu.json)
(`lms get unsloth/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-GGUF@iq4_xs`, apply the preset, load `@iq4_xs`). ~100–103 tok/s,
coherent output (verified: correct arithmetic + reasoning), 0.86 MTP accept on structured prompts.
IQ4_XS (~20 GB VRAM) leaves the most headroom for larger context; Q4_K_S (~27 GB) is marginally
faster. That's **~3.8× the as-found baseline** (27.4 tok/s: untuned Q8, 256k, parallel 4, spilling
~11 GB to RAM).

**The quant curve is steep here (opposite of the 8 GB box).** Because decode is GPU-bandwidth-bound
on the resident weights, smaller/faster quants win and the "best quality Q4" is *not* the fastest:

| quant | VRAM | tok/s | note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Q4_K_S | 27 GB | 103 | fastest that's still solid quality |
| Q2_K_XL | 20 GB | 103 | as fast, lowest fidelity — only for max context headroom |
| IQ4_XS | 25 GB | 100 | **sweet spot** — imatrix 4-bit, best quality-per-byte, headroom |
| Q4_K_XL | 27 GB | 84 | UD keeps more layers high-precision → ~20% more GPU bytes/token |
| Q8_0 (hybrid) | spills | 33 | doesn't fit → experts stream from RAM (see below) |

**The x1 card helps full-residency but hurts hybrid.** With the model fully in VRAM, llama.cpp
layer-splits and only a tiny per-token hidden state crosses the x1 link (GPU1 ran Gen4 x1 at ~49%
util, no bottleneck) — so the second card is pure extra VRAM. But at **Q8 the model doesn't fit**, so
experts stream from system RAM, and any streamed expert feeding the x1 card is throttled: Q8 hybrid
managed only ~33 tok/s (worse than the single-16GB-GPU hybrid anchor of 45.9). **If you need Q8
fidelity, this x16+x1 split is the wrong tool** — stay at Q4-class-in-VRAM, or move the second card
off the x1 slot onto a wider link.

Levers that didn't matter here: `cpu_experts` ratio (irrelevant once everything's resident);
KV→GPU (always on, room to spare). Context is retunable later; 128k was the target and fits with room.

**Concurrency: turn MTP OFF to serve multiple users.** MTP is a single-stream win but caps aggregate
throughput under load — its draft/verify structure won't batch. Measured (IQ4_XS, 32k, parallel 4,
VRAM ~22 GB, no spill):

All figures are **decode tok/s** (same metric as the single-stream table): *total* = combined across
all streams, *per stream* = what each concurrent user sees.

| streams | MTP on — total | MTP on — per stream | MTP off — total | MTP off — per stream |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 105 | 105 | 87 | 87 |
| 2 | 89 | 44 | 136 | 68 |
| 4 | 100 | 25 | 180 | 45 |
| 8 | — | — | **202** | 25 |

With MTP **on**, total decode is flat (~90–100) and just divided among streams — batching gains
nothing. With MTP **off**, total scales toward the MoE-bandwidth ceiling: 87 → 136 → 180 → 202, with
**diminishing returns** (+56% → +32% → +12%) flattening near ~200. Each stream is also faster under
contention than with MTP on. VRAM held ~22 GB through parallel 8 (no spill). So: **single user → MTP
on (~105 tok/s); multi-user serving → MTP off, parallel 4–8 (180–202 total, 25–45 tok/s per user).**
The old spilling was Q8 or big-context×parallel, not concurrency itself. (The 8 GB box stayed flat at
~20 total either way — RAM-bandwidth-capped.)

## gpt-oss-120b (117B / 5.1B active, MXFP4 experts, 63.4 GB)

**Recommended: hybrid, `cpu_experts=0.61`, 32k ctx, flash on, KV q8_0 on GPU, threads 8,
`keep_in_ram=false`, `mmap=true` → ~21 tok/s decode, ~326 tok/s prefill (34 s per 16k prompt).**
Preset: [`presets/gpt-oss-120b-hybrid-dualgpu.json`](../presets/gpt-oss-120b-hybrid-dualgpu.json).
Coherence verified (arithmetic + factual). No MTP head; MTP off.

**Quants are a dead end for this model:** experts (~99% of bytes) are native MXFP4 and don't
requantize — the unsloth GGUFs run 62.6 GB (Q2_K) to 63.4 GB (Q8_0), <2% apart. Expert *placement*
is the only lever on this box.

**The cpu_experts ladder is shallow** (each ~4 layers ≈ 6.5 GB of VRAM buys ~1 tok/s), because the
CPU-resident experts dominate per-token time — decode stays RAM-bandwidth-bound at every ratio:

| cpu_experts | expert layers on GPU | VRAM | decode tok/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00 | 0 | 4.9 GB | 17.3 |
| 0.89 | 4 | 9.7 GB | 18.0 |
| 0.78 | 8 | 15.8 GB | 19.3 |
| 0.67 | 12 | 22.2 GB | 20.6 |
| 0.61 | 14 | 25.5 GB | **21.2** |

LM Studio **clamps at 14 expert layers** here — ratios below 0.61 (0.58, 0.55) load byte-identical
placements, so 0.61 is the effective floor. Concurrency (at 0.67, parallel 8, 32k): total decode
20.5 → 27.7 → 35.8 → 40.2 tok/s at 1/2/4/8 streams — ~2× at 8 streams, per-stream 5 tok/s.

**⚠ OOM post-mortem (this box hard-locked once on this model).** The crash config was
ctx=120000 + cpu_experts=0.583 + **no flash attention**: ~25 GB of experts + 120k KV + a huge
non-flash attention scratch buffer overflowed VRAM, and the CUDA sysmem fallback committed the
overflow into host RAM on top of ~35 GB of mmap'd experts — commit charge blew past RAM+pagefile
and Windows froze. Rules for 60 GB-class hybrids on a 64 GB box: flash **on** always; big ctx and
low cpu_experts are a multiplicative VRAM cost — change one at a time; `keep_in_ram=false` +
`mmap=true` so CPU-side weights stay reclaimable; run `ramguard.ps1` (commit-floor watchdog)
during loads. Note: free *physical* RAM near zero during load/prefill is normal mmap behavior and
harmless — commit headroom is the signal that matters.

**Where this lands:** ~5× slower than qwen3.6-35B IQ4_XS fully-resident (~100 tok/s) on the same
box. Usable for single-user chat/agent work where the capability jump is worth 21 tok/s; prefill
makes big-context agentic loops painful (34 s per 16k of fresh prompt; 128k would be several
minutes). 128k ctx untested — would need cpu_experts backed off ~2 layers for KV; retune if needed.

## Max resident context: Coder-30B IQ4_XS tops out at 176k (GPU1 re-enabled 2026-07-17)

**Question: how much context can the fastest model on this box carry without leaving the
fully-resident regime? Answer: 176k (180224), and it's free — 122.3 tok/s decode, 3539 tok/s
prefill (3.1 s per 11k), statistically identical to the 32k/128k numbers.** Config: cpu_experts=0,
flash on, KV q8_0, parallel 1, threads 8. The ladder, all fully-resident attempts:

| ctx | GPU0/GPU1 after load | decode tok/s (3 runs) | verdict |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 128k | 13.4 / 11.6 GB | 117.9 stable | fine (re-baseline after GPU1 re-enable) |
| 160k | 14.6 / 12.6 GB | 117.8 stable | fine |
| **176k** | **15.1 / 13.1 GB** | **122.3 stable** | **winner — ~1 GB GPU0 headroom** |
| 192k | 15.8 / 13.5 GB | 118 → 89 → 84 | soft spill: loads, degrades run-over-run |
| 224k | 16.0 / 14.5 GB | ~0 (31 tok in 10 min) | thrash at the VRAM ceiling; had to kill |
| 256k | — | — | load rejected (strict VRAM cap) — native max unreachable |

The failure mode is graded, not a cliff: ~470 MiB free on GPU0 still "works" but decays as other
processes nibble VRAM; at the ceiling it wedges at near-zero GPU utilization. Rule of thumb from
this ladder: **keep ≥1 GB free on the fuller card.** Caveat: decode was measured with a short
prompt (empty cache); decode *at* a filled 176k context will be lower (attention cost), but the
residency boundary is what was mapped here. `gpuStrictVramCap=true` made 256k fail loudly instead
of silently spilling — keep it on.

Two ops gotchas from the GPU1 re-enable, both of which silently faked a single-GPU box:
1. **LM Studio persists its own per-GPU disable list** (`.internal/hardware-config.json`,
   `load.gpuSplitConfig.disabledGpus`). Re-enabling the card in Device Manager is NOT enough —
   the first "dual-GPU" bench ran entirely on GPU0 at 50.8 tok/s until GPU1 was re-enabled in
   LM Studio's hardware settings too. Sanity-check `nvidia-smi` per-GPU memory after every load.
2. `lms server stop/start` does NOT re-enumerate GPUs (engine process outlives it).

## KV-cache quant unlocks native 256k on Coder-30B — but only symmetric K/V

Follow-up to the 176k ceiling above: quantizing the **KV cache** (not the weights) to q4_0 fits
the full native 262144 context fully resident — **114.2 tok/s decode, 3663 tok/s prefill, 1.6/3.7
GB headroom**. Config pinned as the model's default: ctx 256k, K q4_0 / V q4_0, flash on,
cpu_experts=0. Quality cost of q4 KV unmeasured here (coherence tests pending).

| K / V cache | max resident ctx | decode | prefill |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| q8_0 / q8_0 | 176k | 122.3 | 3539 |
| q4_0 / q4_0 | **256k (native max)** | 114.2 | 3663 |
| q8_0 / q4_0 | n/a | ~48 at ANY ctx | times out |

**Trap: mixed K/V quant types are silently broken on this build** (llama.cpp CUDA 12 backend,
LM Studio 2026-07). K q8_0 + V q4_0 loads fine, VRAM shows resident with headroom, but decode
pins at ~48 tok/s with >50% CPU and 16k prefill times out (>240 s) — at 224k AND 256k alike.
That's the flash-attention kernel falling back to a CPU path for asymmetric KV types, not a VRAM
spill: identical ~48 tok/s signature regardless of headroom. If you see fast-ish decode + hammered
CPU + dead prefill, check for mismatched kcache/vcache before blaming memory.

## Laguna family (poolside, arch `laguna`): 1:3 global-to-SWA attention, 256k native

**Recommended: `laguna-xs-2.1` Q4_K_M, fully GPU-resident at the FULL 262144 context, KV q4_0 —
103.5 tok/s decode, 3652 tok/s prefill (3.0 s per 10.8k), 256 tok/s aggregate at 8 streams.**
Config: `cpu_experts=0`, flash on, **kcache=q4_0 + vcache=q4_0**, threads 8, parallel 1 (or 4–8 for
serving). Coherent at q4 KV (47×38=1786, bat-and-ball $0.05). The 118B `laguna-s-2.1` also runs
here but is **not recommended** — see below.

Both models are supported by stock **llama.cpp 2.27.1**; the model card's claim that you need
poolside's fork is wrong for LM Studio. Cheap pre-flight before downloading tens of GB:
`grep -a -o -i laguna llama.dll` in the backend dir — the arch strings are compiled in.

**Why full context is affordable here: only 1 layer in 4 scales with it.** Both models use a 1:3
global-to-sliding-window layout (window 512), 8 KV heads, head dim 128 — so the SWA layers hold a
fixed 512 tokens no matter the context, and only the global layers grow:

| | blocks | global / SWA | KV @ 262144, q8_0 | vs a conventional model of the same depth |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Laguna-S | 48 | 12 / 36 | ~7.2 GB | ~29 GB |
| Laguna-XS | 40 | 10 / 30 | ~6.0 GB | ~24 GB |

`context_length` in the GGUF is **262144**, not the 1M the model card advertises (rope is YaRN ×32
over an 8192 base = exactly 262144). Treat 256k as the real ceiling.

### ⚠ The number that matters: decode collapses with context DEPTH, on both models

A normal sweep row reports decode with an empty cache, and for these models that is a fiction.
Measured with real filled caches at the recommended config for each:

| real prompt tokens | Laguna-S (hybrid) | Laguna-XS (resident) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| ~106 (empty — what a sweep reports) | 16.30 | **103.5** |
| 43,136 | 8.16 | 27.25 |
| 86,214 | 4.10 | 16.12 |
| 242,377 (genuine full context) | **1.56** | **6.49** |

Decode falls roughly linearly with depth — the 12/10 global layers do full attention over the whole
cache, while the SWA layers stay flat (which is why it's a ~16× fall and not the ~100× a
conventional model would take). **"Loads at 256k" is not "usable at 256k."** Budget by the depth you
actually work at: XS is comfortable to ~40–80k and degrades gracefully; S is single-digit tok/s past
~80k. This is the same trap flagged for the Coder-30B 176k ceiling above, but here it is a 16×
effect, so it is worth re-measuring for any long-context model rather than trusting an empty-cache row.

### Ops gotchas found while benchmarking these

1. **`lms get` cannot resolve either repo** (`unsloth/Laguna-S-2.1-GGUF`, `poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1-GGUF`)
   — "artifact does not exist or you do not have permission". Download with `curl` straight into

2. **Killing a sweep does not kill `ladder.sh`/`sweep.sh`.** The script survived its parent and

   historical is invalid, but prefill tok/s is not comparable across tokenizers, and any
   "decode at Nk" probe needs its real token count read back from `prompt_tokens`.

## fiveforge llama.cpp fork (wp-c3 tensor-parallel): cannot help on THIS box — don't re-test here

**Verdict: the `wp-c3` multi-GPU optimisations cannot engage on this machine, and would be a
pessimisation if they could. Test them on a box with real P2P (the V100 rig), not here.**
Established without needing to run a binary — three independent blockers:

**1. These two GPUs have no CUDA peer-to-peer.** Direct probe (`cudaDeviceCanAccessPeer` +
`cudaDeviceEnablePeerAccess`, both directions):

```
canAccessPeer 0->1 : 0        canAccessPeer 1->0 : 0
enablePeerAccess   : "peer access is not supported between these two devices"
```

Consumer GeForce under the Windows WDDM driver model does not expose P2P. The fork's one-shot
allreduce (`GGML_CUDA_ONESHOT_AR`) self-disables on exactly this failure — see the
`"oneshot AR disabled: cudaDeviceEnablePeerAccess(...) failed"` warning in `ggml-cuda.cu`.

**2. The threaded path needs NCCL, which has no Windows build.** `GGML_META_THREADS` (per-device
worker threads with per-rank NCCL allreduce) depends on `find_package(NCCL)`; without it the build
prints *"NCCL not found, performance for multiple CUDA GPUs will be suboptimal"* and the path is
compiled out.

**3. Even working, tensor-parallel is the wrong shape for an x1 riser.** Measured host-staged peer
copy cost on this box: **63.5 µs for a 4 KB decode-sized message, 1.68 GB/s at 4 MB.** The two split
modes use the link completely differently:

| split mode | link crossings per token | cost per token | share of a 9.7 ms XS token |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `-sm layer` (what we run) | 1 | ~0.06 ms | **0.7%** |
| `-sm tensor` (TP) | ~80 (2 allreduces × 40 layers) | ~5–10 ms | **dominates** |

TP trades VRAM-splitting for per-layer collectives. On this box the second card is on a **Gen1 x1**
link, and the existing notes already conclude it is "pure extra VRAM" *precisely because* layer-split
barely touches it. TP would convert the link from a non-issue into the bottleneck. The fork's
multi-GPU work came out of the V100 TP campaign — V100s on Linux have working P2P and NCCL, a
genuinely different machine.

**Arch support is NOT the blocker** (checked, in case it comes up): `llm_arch_supports_sm_tensor()`
is a **denylist** — it returns false for listed archs and **true by default** — and `LLM_ARCH_LAGUNA`
is not listed, so `-sm tensor` is available for both Laguna models. The fork also ships
`src/models/laguna.cpp`.

**Ops blocker if you do try to run a local build here: Smart App Control.** The fork builds fine
(CUDA 13.2 + MSVC 2022 BuildTools + Ninja, `-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=120`), but every freshly
compiled unsigned binary is blocked at launch: *"An Application Control policy has blocked this
file"*. `VerifiedAndReputablePolicyState = 1` (enforcing). **Turning Smart App Control off is a
one-way door — it cannot be re-enabled without reinstalling Windows** — so do not disable it for a
benchmark. WSL2 (Ubuntu 24.04, both GPUs visible) sidesteps it, but ships `nvcc 12.0`, which cannot
target sm_120 (Blackwell needs 12.8+), and has no cmake — budget a ~4 GB toolkit install. P2P is
absent inside WSL too, so it would only confirm the negative.

A build worktree was left at `repos/third-party/llama.cpp-wp-c3` (detached at `bedc46309`); remove
with `git worktree remove llama.cpp-wp-c3` from the main clone. The primary checkout's branch was
never touched.

42 °C, ~40% of the 180 W limit, and 2850–2857 MHz against a 3090 MHz ceiling — nothing a board
partner controls (cooler, VRM, factory OC, power limit) is binding. A driver/kernel change fits
better. Untestable now: the Asus is out of the box.

**The `machine_id` will not change itself.** `_spec_slug()` derives the id from CPU + GPU count +
GPU name + VRAM only — not PCIe topology or board vendor — and `ensure_registered()` writes
`machines/<id>.json` just `if not os.path.exists(path)`, so stored specs are never refreshed on
existing hardware. Before the next GPU change: delete `.machine_id` to mint a fresh one, or add
drift detection. Adding driver + LM Studio version to `detect_specs()` would make the next swap
answerable instead of guesswork.

**Diagnostic note:** `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=pcie.link.gen.max` folds in the *slot's* negotiated
ceiling — it reads Gen4 x8 on GPU1, which is the motherboard's x1 riser, not a card limit. Compare
cards with `pcie.link.gen.gpumax` / `Device Max` (Gen5 on both). VBIOS strings also differ between
production batches of one SKU (`98.06.1f.00.d6` vs `98.06.4e.80.25` here), so they are not model
identity either.
